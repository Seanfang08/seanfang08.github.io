Data-driven Analysis of Chemical Short-range Order-Property Correlations in Multi-principal Element Alloys
==============================================================================================================

Overview
--------
This study explores how chemical short-range order (SRO) patterns relate to
material properties in multi-principal element (medium-entropy) alloys using a
data-driven workflow. We assembled features that capture pair/cluster
preferences, trained and compared models, and examined which SRO descriptors
most strongly correlate with measured properties.

Key Results
-----------
- Demonstrated a practical pipeline from SRO feature extraction to predictive
  modeling and interpretation.
- Identified influential SRO descriptors associated with property variation in
  selected alloy systems.
- Showed that simple, well-regularized models can provide competitive accuracy
  and clearer interpretability for scientific insight.


Presentations
-------------
+ **S. Fang**, Data-driven Analysis of Chemical Short-range Order-Property
  Correlations in Multi-principal Element Alloys, Contributed Talk, Joint
  Mathematics Meetings, Seattle, WA., Jan. 2025

Research Story
--------------
This work set out to connect chemical short-range order patterns with measured
material properties using a clear, data-driven pipeline. We gathered SRO
descriptors that reflect pair and cluster preferences, standardized the data,
and compared baseline models with strong regularization. Iterating on features
and diagnostics (residuals, cross-validation), we focused on interpretability
and reproducibility as much as raw accuracy.

Through successive experiments, a consistent picture emerged: a small subset of
SRO descriptors carried most of the explanatory power, and simpler models made
those relationships easier to inspect. The result is a practical baseline for
future work—where more complex models can build on well-understood features and
validation checks—while keeping the conclusions accessible to a broad audience.
